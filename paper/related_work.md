## 1. Multi-camera Panorama Stitching for Autonomous Vehicles

Classical panorama stitching (Brown and Lowe, AutoStitch [CITE]; Szeliski's image alignment survey [CITE]) assumes input images come from a freely-moving camera with a near-common optical center and large pairwise overlap, conditions that make the parallax error of treating the scene as on a distant sphere acceptable. Autonomous-vehicle (AV) ring rigs violate every one of these assumptions: the seven AV2 ring cameras (Wilson et al., NeurIPS Datasets 2023 [CITE]) sit on a ~2-meter-wide platform, share only ~10-20% overlap between adjacent cams, observe scene content as close as 1.5 m from the rig (so a 1-degree mis-projection translates to centimeter-scale ghosting on lane markings), and span both pinhole and fisheye lens models — front-center, side, and rear cameras all have different focal lengths and distortion profiles. As a result, sphere-projection baselines (our L1) suffer characteristic 5-20 cm cross-camera misalignment on ground-plane content even when the camera extrinsics are calibrated to within 1 cm.

AV-specific approaches have begun to address this. **OmniStitch** [CITE ACM MM 2024] introduces a depth-aware stitching network trained on the synthetic **GV360** dataset, but its real-world AV transfer remains under-evaluated and the model architecture is closed-source. **LiftProj** [CITE arXiv 2512.24276] uses DUSt3R-style lifting followed by cylindrical projection but inherits DUSt3R's reference-frame dependence, which is structurally awkward for AV rigs where no single camera is privileged. Our position: 3D scene representation should serve as both a *stitching aid* (e.g., as a ground-plane prior, which we operationalize via IPM) and as an *output for downstream* (Pantheon360-style 360° video diffusion), rather than as a hard replacement for sphere projection. We document the failure mode of using lifted depth as a direct forward-splat (Section 4.X) and motivate the hybrid design.

## 2. Monocular / Multi-view 3D Reconstruction for Driving

The DUSt3R [CITE Wang et al. CVPR 2024] / MASt3R [CITE] lineage proposed feed-forward 3D reconstruction from un-posed image collections, treating one input as the reference frame and predicting all other views' point maps in its coordinate system. While powerful for unstructured Internet photos, the reference-frame dependence is a structural weakness for AV rigs: rear-cam reconstruction degrades badly when the reference is a front cam. **Pi3** [CITE Wang et al., ICLR 2026 claimed] removes this dependence by adopting a permutation-equivariant transformer architecture, and is our chosen backbone for that reason. We provide the first AV-domain quantitative evaluation of Pi3 against LiDAR ground truth, finding abs_rel = 0.202 ± 0.042 across 10 anchors and δ<1.25 = 0.866 on the best anchor (comparable to KITTI-tuned Monodepth2 [CITE]).

Alternative monocular metric depth backbones — **Depth Pro** [CITE Bochkovskii et al. arXiv 2024], **Metric3D v2** [CITE Yin et al.], **Depth Anything V2** [CITE Yang et al.] — provide single-image depth but lack Pi3's joint multi-view conditioning and would need separate cross-camera alignment. **Fin3R** [CITE NeurIPS 2025 claimed, arXiv 2511.22429] proposes a LoRA fine-tune of Pi3/DUSt3R using Depth-Anything-V2 as a monocular teacher, which is adjacent and complementary to our self-supervised cycle-PSNR fine-tune direction (Section 6 future work). We characterize Pi3's depth-dependent systematic bias (-10.2 ± 11.2 % at <5 m monotonically growing to -23.7 ± 6.8 % at 40-60 m) and discuss its implications for downstream 3D-aware consumers.

## 3. Panoramic Video Generation

360° video generation is an active 2025-2026 area. **Pantheon360** [CITE] (Koi's target downstream consumer in our pipeline) targets 360° video diffusion conditioned on a 3D cache, building on the **GEN3C** [CITE] 3D-cache-conditioned video diffusion paradigm. **Argus / Beyond the Frame** [CITE] extends perspective video to 360°. **Panacea+** [CITE arXiv 2408.07605] is — to our knowledge — the only AV2-evaluated 360° video generation method, providing a direct baseline for our pipeline's output quality.

Recent panoramic-native diffusion methods include **PanFlow** [CITE AAAI 2025 claimed] (spherical noise warping with decoupled camera rotation) and **VideoPanda** [CITE NVIDIA arXiv 2504.11389] (text/video-conditioned 360° diffusion). The closest direct competitor to our line is **Percep360** [CITE ICRA 2026 claimed, arXiv 2507.06971], which proposes Local Scenes Diffusion + Probabilistic Prompting for AV → 360° generation; their approach is diffusion-only with no explicit 3D scene step, making it orthogonal-but-adjacent to our 3D-aware hybrid. **CylinderSplat** [CITE ICLR 2026 claimed, arXiv 2603.05882] introduces feed-forward 3DGS with a cylindrical triplane representation natural to panoramic novel-view synthesis, suggesting an avenue beyond our current point-cloud + ERP output.

Our position: 3D-scene-input to 360° video diffusion is the missing piece between feed-forward 3D reconstruction (Pi3) and panoramic video generation (Pantheon360 / Percep360). We provide that missing AV2 adaptation layer.

## 4. Evaluation Protocols

In the absence of a ground-truth 360° AV dataset (no AV2 log provides a true equirectangular ground-truth image since the rigs do not carry 360° cameras), prior work has used **cycle-consistency PSNR / SSIM** [CITE leave-one-out reprojection convention] as a no-GT proxy: hold out one input view, reconstruct it from the panorama via inverse projection, and PSNR against the held-out original. We extend this protocol along three axes. First, we report PSNR alongside **MS-SSIM** [CITE Wang et al. multi-scale SSIM] and **LPIPS** [CITE Zhang et al. CVPR 2018] to forestall the "PSNR favors blurry outputs" critique, finding all three metrics rank methods identically. Second, we **stratify PSNR by spatial region** (sky / object / ground rows) to identify where parallax-driven errors live (the object band shows the largest method-vs-method gap at -6.88 dB for naive 3D lift vs sphere). Third, we **anchor depth quality against LiDAR sweeps** using the AV2 LiDAR (10 Hz, sync-delta < 50 ms), reporting standard monocular-depth metrics (abs_rel, δ<1.25, RMSE) and an additional **depth-binned bias** breakdown that exposes the monotonic compression of Pi3's far-field estimates.

The **multi-anchor stress test** (N = 10 anchor frames per log, evenly spaced across a 16-second sequence) is our methodological contribution to AV-360 evaluation: it quantifies per-frame variance in stitching/depth quality and makes single-anchor results falsifiable. We find Phase-2 single-frame headline numbers all fall within 1σ of the 10-anchor mean, validating prior single-frame practice while exposing the previously-uncharacterized scene-dependent quality variation (best anchor reaches KITTI Monodepth2 SOTA; worst drops to δ<1.25 = 0.41).

## 5. Gaps This Paper Addresses

Despite a maturing landscape, three gaps remain unaddressed:

1. **No published method provides a hybrid 2D/3D pipeline for AV 360° stitching.** OmniStitch is fully neural; sphere projection is fully analytical; LiftProj and Percep360 are 3D-only or diffusion-only. We propose an IPM-ground-prior + sphere hybrid that is analytically grounded for the ground plane (parallax-free by construction) and falls back to sphere projection elsewhere, achieving +0.20 ± 0.11 dB ground-only PSNR with zero full-image regression.

2. **The failure modes of naive Pi3 / DUSt3R forward-splat to ERP have not been systematically characterized.** We document the -3.15 ± 0.72 dB cycle-PSNR gap on 10 anchors, isolate it across PSNR / MS-SSIM / LPIPS / region-PSNR (the gap *widens* on perceptual metrics, ruling out the "PSNR is biased" hypothesis), and decompose it into five mechanisms (depth variance, multi-cam blend doubling, conf-cliff sky holes, dynamic content, depth-dependent bias). This motivates the hybrid design and informs anyone considering a pure-3D-lift approach.

3. **Evaluation rigor for AV-360 has been single-frame and single-metric.** We propose a multi-anchor + multi-metric + region-separated + depth-binned evaluation protocol, demonstrate it on one AV2 log, and release the harness for reproducibility.
